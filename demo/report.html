How it works

1
A buyer and a seller agree on deliverables and acceptance criteria. The buyer locks payment in an Alkahest escrow on Base Sepolia, arbitrated by the official TrustedOracleArbiter.
2
The same agreement and criteria open a case in the DealJudge intelligent contract on GenLayer. The seller delivers with an evidence package: a manifest, a pinned sha256 hash, and a public evidence note.
3
If the buyer accepts, the case resolves to RELEASE with no judging. If the buyer disputes, DealJudge fetches the evidence, verifies the hash, and a leader/validator consensus judges each criterion → RELEASE, REFUND, or UNDETERMINED.
4
A disclosed, trusted manual relay forwards the recorded verdict to the arbiter. RELEASE lets the seller collect; REFUND blocks collection and the buyer reclaims after expiry; UNDETERMINED is never relayed.

Engineering notes — what the chain taught us

Evidence representation matters. DealJudge v3 pinned evidence by sha256 but fetched it with gl.nondet.web.render, which normalizes text (706 raw chars became 703) — the hash could never match. A dedicated on-chain diagnostic contract (RenderProbe) proved it; v4 fetches the raw body with gl.nondet.web.get, gates on HTTP status 200, and hashes the full body.

ABI encoding has two shapes. The Alkahest arbiter decodes the escrow demand as abi.encode(DemandData) — the nested tuple encoding with its outer offset word. Two early escrows used the flat abi.encode(address, bytes) form; their demands cannot be decoded, so they can never be collected (0.002 ETH each, reclaimable by the buyer after expiry ~27 Nov 2026). The corrected escrow above uses the nested form.

Trust assumptions, disclosed. The relay between GenLayer and Base Sepolia is a trusted manual operator step: it forwards only the verdict read from get_case(), never a UI or log claim, and never forces UNDETERMINED. Roles are played by separate testnet accounts per chain (disclosed in demo/run-state.json). Testnets only; no real funds.
